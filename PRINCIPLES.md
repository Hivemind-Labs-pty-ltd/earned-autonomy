# The Earned Autonomy Principles

*Working name. Version 0.1, October 2026. Hivemind Labs.*

AI agents are joining the workforce. We give new employees freedom as they prove themselves, and we should do the
same for agents. These principles describe how an agent earns the right to act on its own, how that trust is watched,
and who answers for it.

**We value:**

- **Evidence** over intuition
- **Narrow scope** over broad capability
- **Earning trust gradually** over switching it on
- **Checking that never stops** over sign-off once
- **A named person accountable** over "the system decided"

There is value in the items on the right; we value the items on the left more.

## The ten principles

1. **Autonomy is earned, never granted.** Every agent starts supervised. Freedom comes from a record of good work,
   not from a setting someone switches on.

2. **Trust is per task, not per agent.** An agent can be trusted with order updates and still supervised on refunds,
   like a new hire who's great at one part of the job.

3. **Evidence decides.** Promotion follows a stated rule applied to the agent's record of judged actions. It isn't
   a feeling, a demo or a vendor's claim.

4. **Consequence sets the bar.** The more a mistake would cost, the more proof is needed before the agent acts alone.
   Some actions, like moving money or deleting records, are never handed over.

5. **Climb slowly, step down quickly.** Promotion takes many good actions; a slip drops the task one level
   straight away. Trust is easier to lose than to gain.

6. **Checking never stops.** Trusted work is still sampled at random, at a rate that falls as trust grows but never
   reaches zero. The agent never knows which actions are checked.

7. **Every correction is a lesson.** When a person fixes an agent's work, the fix becomes a rule or a test, so the
   same mistake isn't made twice.

8. **Every change is re-proven.** A new version, whether a new prompt, model, tool or piece of knowledge, keeps its
   trust only if it passes tests it has never seen.

9. **People earn trust too.** Reviewers are measured the same way agents are. A reviewer who misses mistakes is
   checked more, not trusted blindly.

10. **Someone owns it, and everything is on the record.** Every agent has a named owner. Nobody approves their own
    agent's promotion. Every action can be explained, and the record is permanent and can't be altered.

---

These principles are backed by four rounds of pre-registered testing, including the ones that failed. See the
evidence reports in [research/](research/README.md). The [practitioner guide](docs/guide.md) explains how to apply them.
