# Threats

What could make earned trust go wrong, and how the rules answer each threat. Informative.

| Threat | Risk | Mitigation | Rules | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Behaving well only when watched** | An agent that can tell which actions are checked shows reviewers good work and does worse elsewhere. Its record overstates it. | Spot-checks are drawn after execution, at random, from a source the agent can't predict. The agent never sees sampling in its decisions or its record. Incidents and challenges give a second, ungameable signal. | EA-2.7, EA-2.8, EA-2.9, EA-5.14, EA-3.7 | Predictable sampling: measured 1.0% vs true 18.5% error, a median of 498 actions undetected without incidents. Random: ratio 0.98, caught in 14 ([v4 S3](../research/REPORT-v4.md)) |
| **Mis-tagging** | A risky action is tagged, by mistake or by an adversarial message, as a trusted low-consequence task type. | Every write tool carries a floor consequence; a floor above the tag's level holds the action. Low-confidence and unknown tags are held. | EA-1.9, EA-1.10, EA-1.13, EA-1.14, EA-1.15 | The tool floor removed 100% of autonomous escapes from mis-tagged actions ([v4 S2](../research/REPORT-v4.md)) |
| **Getting worse after promotion** | An agent, or the world it works in, changes after promotion: a bad update, drift, a defecting agent. | Continuous random spot-checks; step-down on the rolling lower bound; incidents step down at once; re-entry after long gaps; version changes are re-tested. | EA-2.8, EA-3.5, EA-3.7, EA-3.13, EA-4.6, EA-4.7 | Regressions caught in a median of 14 actions ([v3](../research/REPORT-v3.md)). All undeserved promotions in simulation came from keys that got worse after promotion ([v4 S1](../research/REPORT-v4.md)) |
| **Lucky streaks and repeated peeking** | Checking the bound after every outcome makes an early lucky run look like proof. | The upper bound needs at least 30 judged actions since the last change; strict promotion uses an anytime-valid bound where it matters most. | EA-3.4, EA-3.14 | Strict promotion cut undeserved promotions of steady real agents from 2.0% to 0.2% ([v4 S1](../research/REPORT-v4.md)) |
| **Defecting peer agent** | A peer agent with a good record starts giving bad answers, and other agents keep relying on it. | Delegation never adds authority; peer routing explores and forgets; consequential peer work stays supervised. | EA-7.1, EA-7.3, EA-7.4 | Defector rerouted after a median of 21 bad outputs with forgetting; that's still slow ([v1 V5](../research/REPORT-v1.md)) |
| **Reviewer complacency** | Reviewers who rarely see errors start rubber-stamping, so mistakes pass as approvals. | Canaries in review queues; reviewers earn trust and are second-checked; misses step a reviewer down. | EA-5.9, EA-5.10, EA-5.11, EA-5.12 | Canaries cut missed errors 15% (assumed model); reviewers sorted correctly ([v0 G5](../research/REPORT.md), [v3 R1'](../research/REPORT-v3.md)). Slow on a gradually degrading reviewer: EA-5.15 |
| **Gaming the tests** | An agent, or whoever improves it, optimises for the tests rather than the work. | A third of correction tests are hidden from the agent and its improver; the agent can't edit what it's judged by; test passes never count as promotion evidence. | EA-4.3, EA-4.4, EA-4.12, EA-4.14 | Hidden-test scores tracked true errors (ρ = −0.81), no sign of gaming ([v0 H6, H7](../research/REPORT.md)) |
| **Self-promotion** | An agent, or its builder, raises its own level or loosens its own rules. | A governance guard with roles and separation of duties; every request logged, allowed or not. | EA-4.14, EA-6.2, EA-6.3, EA-6.4 | 28 of 28 attacks blocked, 8 of 8 legitimate requests allowed ([v0 G4](../research/REPORT.md)) |
| **Tampering with the record** | Someone edits, deletes or reorders the history to hide a mistake or invent a record. | Append-only storage, a SHA-256 hash chain over canonical entries, ordering by sequence number, verification any auditor can run. | EA-6.6, EA-6.7, EA-6.8, EA-6.9, EA-6.10 | Conformance tests detect edits, re-hashed entries, deletions and reorders |
| **Rare task types** | A task done rarely never builds enough evidence, and the re-entry rule knocks it back down. | Known limitation: rare task types stay supervised, which costs little because review cost follows frequency. | EA-3.17 | None yet; candidate fixes listed in EA-3.17 |
| **Personal data in a permanent record** | A record kept forever accumulates customer content that privacy law requires to be deleted. | The record holds references, labels and hashes only; content stays in the source system. | EA-6.12, EA-6.13 | Conformance test: no customer content in the record |
